# ClaimSaver+ — Product Brief for Rebuild Agents

This document is the business source of truth for ClaimSaver+. Read it before designing, writing copy, or implementing a new version of the product.

It describes **what the business is, who it serves, what it does, what it must never claim to do, and how the customer journey and claim workflow actually work.** It does not describe technology, architecture, or implementation.

If any later idea conflicts with this brief—especially with the legal-boundary rules—this brief wins.

---

## 1. What ClaimSaver+ is

**ClaimSaver+ is a self-serve guided software platform that helps Florida drivers prepare, organize, and submit their own motor-vehicle no-fault (PIP) claim materials.**

The company sells **access to tools**, not legal services and not claim handling.

The customer:

- enters their own facts
- uploads their own documents
- tracks their own treatment, expenses, and deadlines
- compiles a package
- **sends that package to their insurance company themselves**

ClaimSaver+ provides:

- guided worksheets modeled on the Florida no-fault application
- field-level explanations and completeness/format checks
- secure document storage and organization
- a personal dashboard, calendar, and reminders
- expense and wage-loss logging
- a printable/exportable summary of what the customer entered
- general educational content about how Florida PIP often works

The customer remains the filer. ClaimSaver+ is never the representative.

**Brand:** ClaimSaver+  
**Live site:** [claimsaverplus.com](https://claimsaverplus.com)  
**Headquarters:** Miami, Florida  
**Languages offered:** English, Spanish, and French

## 3. What ClaimSaver+ is not

This is the most important section of the product. Getting it wrong is not a copy issue; it is an unauthorized-practice-of-law and consumer-protection issue.

ClaimSaver+ is **not**:

- a law firm
- an attorney, paralegal service, or legal clinic
- a public adjuster acting on the customer’s claim (unless a separately licensed, separately contracted product is offered later—see §16)
- the customer’s representative to an insurance company
- a claims adjuster negotiating benefits
- a medical advisor
- a HIPAA-covered health care provider or health plan
- a guaranteed-recovery or “we get you paid” service

ClaimSaver+ does **not**:

- give legal advice
- evaluate whether a claim has legal merit
- tell the customer what they “should” claim
- negotiate with insurers
- speak to an adjuster on the customer’s behalf
- file or submit the claim to the insurer
- guarantee any benefit, payment, settlement, or timeline
- take a percentage of PIP benefits or any other recovery
- create an attorney–client relationship
- analyze a provider’s credentials or decide whether a visit satisfies Florida’s 14-day rule

Support may help with **login, billing, and how to use the website**. Support may not answer “what should I do in my case?”

Whenever a situation needs legal judgment, the product points the customer to a licensed Florida attorney. It does not match them to a lawyer as part of the $500 product.

## 4. Why the product exists

ClaimSaver+ was built from a family experience with accident claims and the cost of traditional help. The founders’ view is that a large share of Florida no-fault filings is paperwork, organization, and deadline awareness—not courtroom strategy—and that people often pay a contingency percentage for work they can complete themselves if the forms and process are made clear.

**Mission:** Give Florida drivers affordable, transparent tools to file and manage no-fault claims, with honest clarity about what software can and cannot do.

**Vision:** Be the trusted alternative for drivers who do not need an attorney for a standard PIP claim—structured organization and education when they choose to manage the filing themselves.

**Values:**

- Transparency about fees and about product limits
- Honesty about what the software cannot do
- Respect for the customer’s autonomy: their claim, their decisions
- Family-first origin: reduce financial burden during an already difficult time

The commercial argument is simple: Florida PIP medical benefits are commonly limited (the marketing comparison uses a **$10,000** example). A typical personal-injury attorney contingency of **33%** on that example is **$3,300**. ClaimSaver+ charges a **flat $500** for software access. On that illustration, the customer keeps more of the benefit.

That comparison is **illustrative only**. It is not a prediction of any customer’s outcome. Copy must always say so.

## 5. Who it is for

**Primary customer:** A Florida driver (or occupant, pedestrian, or cyclist in a motor-vehicle incident) who has a **standard Personal Injury Protection (PIP) / no-fault claim** and wants to prepare and send their own materials without hiring a lawyer for routine paperwork.

Covered incident types the product is designed around:

- car
- motorcycle
- truck
- pedestrian
- bicycle

…when the claim is a **Florida motor-vehicle PIP / no-fault** claim.

**Not the current market:**

- claims in other states (other states may be added later)
- bodily-injury liability lawsuits
- uninsured / underinsured motorist disputes as the core product
- commercial-fleet or complex multi-policy stacking as the core product
- customers who need someone to talk to the insurer for them
- customers whose claim has already become a legal fight

**Signals the customer should leave the DIY path and call a licensed attorney:**

- serious injury, or facts that may leave the no-fault system
- claim denials or coverage disputes
- multiple vehicles or complex liability
- any question that requires legal advice about their specific situation
- examinations under oath, independent medical exams, or recorded statements they do not feel equipped to handle
- overlapping workers’ compensation, Medicare, or stacked coverages they cannot sort out
- fraud allegations or coordinated treatment audits

The product has a dedicated page for this: **When to Call an Attorney**. Do not bury it. Do not pretend every PIP claim belongs on the platform.

## 6. Domain: Florida PIP (what the product is built around)

ClaimSaver+ exists because Florida has a **no-fault** auto insurance system. After a qualifying motor-vehicle accident, **Personal Injury Protection (PIP)** can help pay, subject to the policy:

- reasonable, related medical expenses
- a portion of lost wages under defined conditions
- certain death benefits

PIP is **first-party** coverage: the customer claims against **their own** auto policy (or the applicable PIP policy), not by proving the other driver was at fault.

Typical marketing illustration (not a promise):

- many policies discuss a **$10,000** PIP medical benefit limit
- deductibles, exclusions, and actual limits vary by policy
- the insurer’s official form and the policy contract control, not ClaimSaver+

### 6.1 The 14-day rule (critical educational concept)

Florida PIP law includes a widely discussed timing rule: if qualifying **initial medical services** are not furnished within **14 calendar days** after the motor-vehicle accident, PIP medical benefits for that incident may be limited.

The product must:

- teach this as **general information**
- put it on calendars and reminder templates when the customer has an accident date
- **never** decide whether a particular ER visit, urgent-care visit, or clinic visit “counts”
- **never** tell a customer they have or have not satisfied the rule

Qualifying initial services are often associated with EMS, hospital emergency departments, urgent care, or specific statutory categories of physicians/dentists. Not every practitioner visit counts. That determination is for the customer, their providers, their insurer, and if needed a lawyer.

### 6.2 What a typical PIP file contains

Think of the customer’s file in three layers:

1. **Identity and policy** — declarations page, PIP endorsements, ID cards, proof coverage was in force, named insured, vehicles, claim/file number once assigned
2. **Accident facts** — crash report (HSMV / law enforcement), scene photos, witness information, tow invoices, ambulance run sheets, vehicle information
3. **Benefit proofs** — medical bills and records, wage proofs, mileage/other expense logs, authorizations, correspondence with the carrier

The software helps the customer assemble those layers. It does not certify that the set is complete for any particular insurer.

### 6.3 Common Florida packet pages the worksheet follows

The guided form is a **personal drafting aid** aligned with the common Florida “Application for Florida No-Fault Benefits” packet (the public reference packet is the Florida Auto No-Fault Forms set). **The insurer’s official form always controls.** Customers are expected to copy answers onto the carrier’s paper form or portal, or to keep the worksheet as their own record.

Typical packet pieces:

1. **Application for Florida “No-Fault” Benefits** — claimant, accident, injury, treatment, employment/wages, other insurance
2. **Insurance information disclosure authorization** — who may receive insurance-file information, for how long, with what exclusions
3. **HIPAA-style medical authorization** — who may receive health information, for how long, with what exclusions
4. **OIR-B1-1571 PIP disclosure** — patient and (when applicable) provider acknowledgment used in many Florida PIP contexts

Florida insurance fraud notice (must appear on the application-style worksheet):

> Any person who knowingly and with intent to injure, defraud or deceive any insurance company makes a statement of claim containing any false incomplete or misleading information, is guilty of a felony of the third degree.

The customer certifies that information they enter is truthful to the best of their knowledge.

### 7.2 What $500 includes

- guided PIP-related information entry (the eight-step worksheet)
- draft auto-save so work is not lost
- secure upload and organization of documents
- field validation and formatting / completeness checks
- expense logging and deadline reminders (general)
- educational articles (not case-specific advice)
- a dashboard with claim progress, documents, and calendar
- a PDF/summary the customer can download, print, and send themselves
- email support for platform and billing questions

### 7.3 What $500 does not include

- representation
- negotiation
- ClaimSaver+ communicating with the insurer
- legal strategy
- medical advice
- a determination of coverage
- notarization (sold separately)
- attorney referral as a bundled service

### 7.4 How to talk about savings

Allowed:

- show **illustrative math** on a $10,000 PIP example vs. a 33% contingency ($3,300)
- say a flat $500 software fee **can leave more of the benefit** on a standard PIP claim the customer manages themselves
- always label the comparison as **illustrative, not a guarantee**

Not allowed:

- “keep 95% of your claim”
- “we recover more money for you”
- “maximum recovery guaranteed”
- implying ClaimSaver+ processes or wins the claim
- treating $10,000 or 33% as the customer’s actual numbers

Example difference shown in marketing: **$3,300 − $500 = $2,800** on the example only.

### 7.5 Payment and after purchase

Payment is processed by Stripe. After a successful payment, the customer receives a receipt and can use the dashboard and guided forms. ClaimSaver+ does not store full card numbers as part of the customer-facing product story.

Refunds, charge disputes, and billing questions go to support. Support still cannot advise on claim merits.

## 8. End-to-end customer journey

This is the intended business flow. A rebuild should preserve it.

### Stage A — Learn (public site, no purchase required)

Public pages explain the product, the fee, and the limits:

- **Home** — Florida PIP, keep what’s yours, not a law firm, $500, five-step path
- **Who We Are** — origin story, mission, values; founder 6-20 license G279764 is noted in the site footer for DFS lookup
- **How It Works** — five steps the customer performs, plus what the platform includes; optional notarization called out as separate
- **Pricing** — $500 vs. illustrative contingency math; checkout
- **Learning Center** — general PIP education
- **When to Call an Attorney** — off-ramps for complex claims
- **Notarization** — optional DocuSign add-on
- **Contact** — platform/technical support only
- **Data handling, Privacy, Terms** — what is collected, what is not promised

A legal disclaimer must be easy to find: ClaimSaver+ is a guided claim technology platform, not a law firm; the customer is the filer.

### Stage B — Create an account

The customer signs up and signs in. Account information must be accurate. They are responsible for credentials.

Starting a claim from marketing pages typically sends an unauthenticated user to sign in, then onward to the claim form or dashboard.

### Stage C — Pay for platform access

The customer buys the $500 guided platform (and optionally notarization). Payment unlocks the workspace. The product is access, not a case being “accepted.”

### Stage D — Work in the dashboard

The dashboard is the customer’s claim workspace:

1. Fill the claim worksheet (eight guided steps, auto-save)
2. Upload documents (police report, medical records, insurance card, photos, bills)
3. Add calendar milestones and reminders
4. Track treatment and expenses
5. When ready, compile and **the customer** sends the package to the insurer
6. The customer handles follow-up with the carrier

Onboarding for a new empty workspace should point to those first three actions.

### Stage E — The insurer’s process (outside ClaimSaver+)

After the customer submits, **the insurance company** reviews materials. Timelines vary. ClaimSaver+ does not control:

- adjuster response time
- medical treatment
- whether benefits are paid
- legal deadlines, except as general reminder templates the customer can use

The customer communicates with the insurer. ClaimSaver+ does not.

## 10. The guided Florida no-fault worksheet (core product)

The claim form is an **eight-step wizard** titled **Florida No-Fault Form**. It is software and storage, not legal advice or representation.

Drafts auto-save so the customer can leave and return. Saving to the account does **not** send anything to an insurer.

### Step 1 — How they will use the worksheet

The customer chooses a completion method:

- they plan to **transfer answers onto the insurer’s official form or portal**, or print the summary for their records; or
- they expect to **fill the paper form the insurer sent by hand**, and may upload a scan later

They are told this is not legal advice and does not tell them what to claim.

### Step 2 — Application header (typical page 1)

Modeled on “Application for Florida No-Fault Benefits”:

- date they are filling it out
- policy holder
- date of accident
- file / claim number if already assigned

Fraud notice is shown here.

### Step 3 — Claimant identity and contact

Typical fields:

- name, email, phone (home and business)
- address
- date of birth
- Social Security number (because the official packet often asks; treat as highly sensitive)
- how long they have lived in Florida
- permanent address if different
- vehicle year, make, model, license plate (also collected as part of identifying the accident vehicle)

### Step 4 — Accident

- date and time
- place
- brief description of the accident and vehicles involved
- the customer’s vehicle and any family vehicle involved

### Step 5 — Injury and medical treatment

- whether they were injured as a result of the accident
- injury description
- whether treated by a doctor; doctor name and address
- hospital inpatient / outpatient; hospital name and address
- medical bills to date
- whether they expect more medical expense

If they were **not** injured, many official paper forms ask them to sign and return early. The worksheet should not invent a different legal rule; it should note that they should follow the insurer’s version of the form.

### Step 6 — Employment, wages, other coverage

- whether the accident was in the course of employment
- whether they lost wages; amount of loss to date; average weekly wage; disability start/end
- workers’ compensation and amount if any
- other expenses
- list of employers as the packet often requests
- health/medical insurance name and member ID (coordination of benefits)

### Step 7 — Supplemental authorizations (typical packet pages 2–3)

Two drafting aids plus PIP disclosure:

**Insurance information disclosure authorization**

- insured name, policy number, company
- complete claim file vs. limited disclosure
- exclusions
- form of disclosure
- reason
- recipient name / organization / address
- duration (dates or until an event)
- who may receive a revocation
- optional signature and date for the customer’s copy

**HIPAA-style medical authorization**

- patient name, health care provider
- complete vs. limited disclosure and exclusions
- recipient, reason, duration, revocation path
- optional signature and date for the customer’s copy

**OIR-B1-1571-style PIP disclosure**

- patient name, signature, date
- provider name, signature, date when applicable

These sections **organize information in the account**. They do not, by themselves, send releases to anyone. The customer chooses what to submit and how. They should use the same titles as their carrier’s pages when they file.

### Step 8 — Review, optional signature, save

The customer reviews answers, may add a signature for their PDF copy (or sign only the insurer’s paper), and saves to their ClaimSaver+ account.

Success message meaning: **information is stored in their account**. They still must send materials to the insurer themselves.

After save they can:

- go to the dashboard
- download or print a PDF summary
- upload that PDF (or other files) into their document vault

Claim numbers are assigned in the pattern of an internal reference (for example `CS` + year/month + a short number). That number is ClaimSaver+’s record ID. It is **not** the insurance company’s claim number unless the customer later enters the insurer’s file number.

## 11. Dashboard and ongoing claim management

The dashboard is titled as a **recovery / claim workspace**, not as “we are handling your case.”

### 11.1 Claims list

The customer can have one or more claim records. Each shows things like status, accident date, location, and estimated value they entered. Statuses used internally include pending, reviewing, approved, rejected, in progress, and completed. Those labels are **workspace organization**. They are not an insurer decision unless the customer is recording what the insurer told them.

Priority labels (low / medium / high / urgent) are likewise internal organization.

### 11.2 Document vault

Customers upload, preview, download, share (exports they choose to create), and delete files.

Suggested document types in the product:

- medical
- legal
- insurance
- evidence
- other

Documents customers are told they will typically need:

- police / crash reports
- medical records and itemized bills
- insurance information and declarations
- photos of the scene and vehicle damage
- wage proofs when claiming lost income
- correspondence with the carrier

ClaimSaver+ does not professionally review evidence for legal sufficiency.

### 11.3 Calendar and reminders

Customers create events of types such as:

- appointment
- deadline
- follow-up
- payment
- custom

They can mark events complete.

When an accident date is known, the product can suggest **Florida PIP–oriented milestone templates** (general information, not legal advice):

| Offset from accident | Label | Why it exists in the product |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Day 0 | Accident date | Anchor for all other reminders |
| Day 14 | 14-day treatment window | Initial medical treatment often must begin within 14 days for full PIP eligibility—customer must confirm with policy/professionals |
| Day 30 | PIP application / notice | Written notice timing varies; track the carrier’s requirements |
| Day 35 | Insurer acknowledgement (illustrative) | Many carriers acknowledge after notice; adjuster timelines differ |
| Day 60 | Medical bills & records | Gather and submit as treatment continues |
| Day 90 | Payment / denial (illustrative) | Processing after bills varies; follow up on outstanding items |
| Day 365 | Suit limitation (verify) | Statutes of limitation are fact-specific—consult a licensed attorney |

Critical vs. non-critical is a reminder emphasis, not a legal ranking.

### 11.4 Recovery tracking

The workspace is for the customer to log:

- medical appointments
- treatment-related records they choose to upload
- expenses and amounts they enter
- lost-income figures they report
- property-related documentation they upload

This is personal organization, not medical monitoring and not legal monitoring.

### 11.5 Claim journey checklist (dashboard)

A typical in-app journey:

1. Enter information (required)
2. Organize documents (required)
3. Track treatment and expenses (required)
4. Insurer processes the claim after the customer submits (required; ClaimSaver+ does not do this step)
5. Stay on deadlines with reminders (optional aid)
6. Customer handles follow-up; ClaimSaver+ does not negotiate (optional aid)

### 11.6 Package the customer actually files

When ready, the customer compiles a complete package and **sends it through the channel their insurer requires** (portal, email, fax, or mail). Best practice the education content teaches:

- cover letter or index of exhibits
- keep identical copies
- follow up in writing if no acknowledgement
- put the claim number on every communication
- respond to supplements item by item rather than dumping the whole file again

ClaimSaver+ may help generate a PDF of the worksheet. **Generating a PDF is not filing.**

## 12. Learning Center (education only)

Articles are **general education about Florida PIP**, not legal advice. Each customer’s situation is unique.

Topics:

1. **How to file a PIP claim in Florida (overview)** — policy docs, notice, application, medical and wage proofs, other coverage, how to transmit a package
2. **The 14-day rule and PIP** — why the initial treatment window matters; emergency medical condition as a related concept; do not decide the customer’s facts
3. **Do you need an attorney for every PIP claim?** — many straightforward claims are paperwork; denials, BI/UM, stacked policies, EUO/IME, and litigation are lawyer territory
4. **Documents often used in no-fault claims** — identity/policy, accident facts, benefit proofs; authorizations; correspondence logs
5. **Common mistakes that complicate claims** — delay, incomplete applications, documentation gaps, thin wage proof, ignoring insurer letters, treating the adjuster as a neutral helper, “full and final” payment language, social media, mixed coverage routing

Footer on educational content: ClaimSaver+ is a guided claim platform and does not provide legal advice.

## 13. Optional online notarization

Notarization is **not** part of guided PIP filing.

- Price: about **$25** per document as currently offered
- Delivery: remote online notarization through **DocuSign**
- The customer needs a valid government-issued ID, a device with camera and microphone, and the document (typically PDF)
- Availability depends on the DocuSign notary network—not 24/7 on-demand and not guaranteed instant
- Session length varies

It can be used for affidavits and other documents that need a notary. It is not a ClaimSaver+ legal review of the document.

Do not bundle notarization into the $500 fee in copy or in checkout logic.

## 17. Voice, copy, and UX rules for any rebuild

### Always true

- The customer files. ClaimSaver+ provides tools.
- “Not a law firm” is a product fact, not a footnote to hide.
- Fees are flat and transparent.
- Education is general; the policy and official forms control.
- No outcome guarantees.
- Complex claims get an off-ramp to a licensed Florida attorney.

### Words to prefer

- guided software / guided platform / tools / worksheet / education / you file / you submit / platform access
- illustrative comparison
- general information, not legal advice

### Words and claims to avoid

- legal representation, we handle your claim, we fight the insurance company
- we evaluate your case, free case review, settlement guarantee
- attorney matching as a core feature of the $500 product
- HIPAA certified / HIPAA compliant as a badge (the product stores health-related files customers upload, but it does not claim covered-entity or business-associate status for routine consumer use)
- “bank-level encryption” as marketing slang
- implying notarization is included in $500
- implying reminders are legally sufficient to meet deadlines

### Tone

Calm, clear, adult, Florida-specific. Helpful without sounding like a lawyer. Direct about money. Direct about limits. Family-origin story is real but secondary to the product definition.

The site should work in a modern mobile browser; customers often upload from a phone after an accident.
